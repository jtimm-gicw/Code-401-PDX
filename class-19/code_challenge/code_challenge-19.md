# 🎤 Mock Interviews

### Code 401 · Technical Whiteboard Interview Practice

> **🎯 Goal:** Practice a real-world technical interview with a classmate. You will take turns being the **interviewer** and the **candidate**, solve a coding challenge, and give each other constructive feedback using a standardized rubric.

---

## 📋 Specifications

- Read **all instructions** before beginning.
- Work with a peer. Each person will take a turn as the **interviewer** and the **candidate**.
- The interviewer will evaluate the candidate using the [**Whiteboard Rubric**](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1scthkmARfzAFZrSYAp6LA2coOaoWUWbSzMbtIU4jcHw).
- The interviewer may take notes in an **electronic copy** of the rubric or on a **printed copy**.
- Give the completed rubric to the candidate so they can review their performance.
- **Each candidate submits their own completed rubric**, including the score they received.
- ⏱️ **Each interview has a strict 30-minute time limit.**

> ⚠️ **Important:** Do **not** open or read the interview questions until you have decided who will interview first.

## 👥 Step 1: Assign Your Roles

| Interview Round | Interviewer | Candidate | Time Limit |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Round 1** | Student A | Student B | **30 minutes** |
| **Round 2** | Student B | Student A | **30 minutes** |

**Interviewer responsibilities**

- Present the assigned problem to the candidate.
- Keep track of the 30-minute interview time.
- Observe how the candidate communicates and approaches the problem.
- Record detailed notes and assign points for **every item** on the Whiteboard Rubric.
- Add up the points and record the final score.
- Share the completed rubric with the candidate.

**Candidate responsibilities**

- Ask questions to clarify the problem.
- Explain your thinking as you work through the challenge.
- Use a whiteboard or equivalent workspace to show your solution.
- Walk through examples, test your approach, and discuss complexity.
- Review your rubric and identify strengths and areas for improvement.

## 🧠 Step 3: Structure Your Whiteboard Interview

Follow the steps in the **Whiteboard Rubric** and your class's **example whiteboard layout**. Your work should demonstrate how you arrive at a solution—not just the final code.

Use the following as a helpful interview workflow:

| Stage | What to Demonstrate |
|:--|:--|
| **1. Understand the problem** | Restate the challenge, identify inputs and outputs, and ask clarifying questions. |
| **2. Examples and edge cases** | Work through a simple example and consider unusual inputs. |
| **3. Visualize the solution** | Draw a diagram or sketch the data structure. |
| **4. Explain your algorithm** | Describe a clear, step-by-step plan. |
| **5. Write pseudocode or code** | Build the solution while communicating your decisions. |
| **6. Verify and analyze** | Trace a test case, discuss correctness, and explain **time and space complexity (Big O)**. |

> 💡 **Student tip:** Talk through your reasoning throughout the interview. Interviewers want to understand **how you solve problems**, not just whether you produce working code.

## 📝 Step 4: Complete the Interview Rubric

The **interviewer** must:

1. Record meaningful notes about the candidate's performance.
2. Evaluate **every item** on the [Whiteboard Rubric](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1scthkmARfzAFZrSYAp6LA2coOaoWUWbSzMbtIU4jcHw).
3. Assign a score to each item according to the rubric.
4. Calculate and record the **total score**.
5. Give the completed rubric to the candidate for review.

The **candidate** should read the feedback and reflect on what went well and what can be improved.

## 📤 Step 5: Submission Instructions

**Submit the result of the interview in which you were the candidate.**

Your submission must include:

- **Your evaluated rubric:** Paste a link to the completed Google document or a photo of the printed rubric.
- **Your score:** Ensure that your interviewer recorded your total score.
- **Your reflection:** Comment on your submission with a short summary addressing:
  - **How did the interview go overall?**
  - **What went well?**
  - **What would you like to improve next time?**
